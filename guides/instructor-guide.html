At a glance

A simulation uses up to three screens, all linked online by one six-character session code.

You drive the patient from the instructor screen. The room monitor shows the team only what they have attached. The defib tablet, if used, sends every press back to your event log.

Before the session

Allow 10 minutes to set up the screens and test them before the team arrives.

Equipment

DeviceRoleWhere it goes
Laptop or tabletInstructor screen: you drive the patient from hereOut of the team's sight, ideally where you can see the room
Second screen, TV, projector or deviceRoom monitor: the patient monitor the team watchesWhere a real monitor would be, visible to the whole team
Tablet (optional)DefibrillatorAt the bedside, for Defib Sim or any scenario that needs a defib
Speakers (optional)Alarms and monitor beepsNext to the room monitor

All devices need an internet connection, and any up-to-date web browser. You do not need an account to run the built-in scenarios.

Pre-flight checklist

  • Open wmebemsim.netlify.app on the instructor laptop and note the session code
  • Open the room monitor and tap Tap to Enable Sound
  • Link the defib tablet, if you are using one
  • Check the badge on the instructor screen reads Live and names the linked screens
  • Load your scenario and read its brief
  • Turn the room monitor's volume up and check you can hear an alarm
  • Change one vital sign and check the room monitor follows

Session codes and linking screens

The six-character session code (for example K7PQ3M) links every screen in one simulation. It never uses look-alike characters (no 0/O or 1/I/L), so it is easy to read out. It shows on the setup screen and as "Session ID" in the top right corner.

  • Same code all day: one code can run any number of scenarios with the same group. The linked screens stay linked between scenarios.
  • New code: press New code on the setup screen for a new group. Screens on the old code must join again.

Choosing a scenario

The setup screen has a tab for each way to start. Premade covers most teaching; Defib Sim is for defibrillator skills.

Human factors. Random and Builder can add a challenge: Blindfolded Lead, Silent Team, New Junior, Missing Kit or Distracted Senior. It shows as a badge on your screen so you can brief the right person.

Paediatric scenarios. Tick Show WETFLAG on Monitor on the setup screen to show the weight-based WETFLAG values on the room monitor.

The brief. Loading a Premade, Random or Custom scenario opens a brief before anything starts:

  • Patient Brief: name, history, medicines and allergies
  • Required Equipment
  • Clinical Progression: how the scenario is expected to unfold
  • Key Interventions the team should perform
  • Guidelines & Resources
  • Learning Objectives: what the debrief scores against

Press Start Scenario to begin, or Back to Setup to choose another.

Resume Previous? If the instructor tab was closed during a scenario, the setup screen offers to resume it. The app saves your progress every few seconds.

Simple and Full view

Simple is the default and is remembered on each device. It hides:

  • the drug-timing detail lines in Drugs on board
  • the "+ Invasive" button that attaches IV access, an arterial line and capnography in one go (the separate buttons stay)
  • the pH and K+ tiles
  • the defib's shock-response settings and "Next shock converts to" (one line shows what is set; click it to switch to Full)
  • the Quick Sim preset list (a preset that is running stays on screen)

Nothing is removed in either view.

On a phone, the obs tiles, rhythm, clock and START/PAUSE sit at the top, and the page scrolls.

Running the patient

You mirror what the team does: they act in the room, you press the matching control, and the patient and room monitor respond.

Monitoring and access

The room monitor only shows what the team has attached. Open Monitoring & access to see a chip for each item.

  • Click a chip to attach it (○) or remove it (●). Removing one blanks its trace and number on the room monitor. Every change is logged.
  • Attach puts on the standard four: ECG, SpO2 probe, NIBP cuff and temperature probe. Capnography, the arterial line and IV access are separate, deliberate steps.
  • Remove all takes every monitor off. IV access stays, because it is a route, not a monitor.
  • POC glucose and POC VBG take a one-off reading at that moment. Click again to take a new one.

Vital signs

Your tiles always show the patient's true values, even when the team cannot see them; "not on monitor" means the team cannot see that number yet.

  1. Click a vital's tile.
  2. Type the new value. For ETCO2 you can also pick the capnogram shape: Auto, Obstructive or Force normal.
  3. Under Get there, choose Now, 30 s, 2 min or 5 min for a gradual change.
  4. Press Confirm.

A heart rate you set yourself is kept if you later change the rhythm. ARREST and ROSC reset it.

Blood pressure

The room monitor shows a new BP only when the cuff cycles, as in real life. Press Cycle NIBP when the team asks for one, or tick "Cycle the NIBP cuff so the team sees it" when you set a BP with Now. An arterial line, once attached, shows the BP continuously. Manual Reading shows a one-off cuff reading on the room monitor without changing the patient (for example a spurious reading).

Treatments and drugs

Find the treatment by search or tab and click it. The app then:

  • logs it with the time
  • applies its effect over a realistic time course: onset, peak and wearing off
  • shows it in Drugs on board with its phase (for example ONSET), strength and time left
  • for infusions and oxygen, keeps it running until you click it again; use + and − in Drugs on board to change an infusion's rate

If the team gives a drug before its prerequisite (for example an IV drug with no IV access), the app records a safety flag. Child doses follow the RCUK paediatric guidance (see Paediatric scenarios).

Investigations

The team can tap 12-Lead, VBG, CXR, Urine, POCUS or CT Head on the room monitor to see results. From the Investigations tab you can also send a result yourself: the scenario's own finding, a quick-select finding or one you type.

Deterioration

Patient condition shows the mode:

  • AUTO: the patient gets worse on their own, at the rate the scenario sets, unless the team treats them.
  • MANUAL: the obs only change when you change them.

Better and Worse move the patient towards the scenario's improved or worsened obs over 30 seconds, in either mode. In many scenarios, giving one of the key treatments starts the improvement by itself, and the log says "Patient condition IMPROVING".

Quick Sim has no scenario, so it leaves out treatments, drugs, deterioration and scoring. Its right side holds the full rhythm list, presets (Full view), Better and Worse, and the event log.

Arrests, rhythms and the defibrillator

All rhythm and resuscitation controls are in the Rhythm & resus section.

ControlWhat it does
ARREST ▾Pick the arrest rhythm. The obs drop to arrest values and the arrest timer starts
ROSC ▾Pick the post-ROSC rhythm. Circulation returns
Change rhythm (above the vitals)Change to any rhythm without an arrest or ROSC, for example sinus to AF
Arrest ViewSwitches the room monitor to the two-trace arrest layout (Lead II and pads). It does not open a defib
DefibOpens a working defibrillator on the room monitor, with the obs still visible, and a defib panel on your screen

The panel under these buttons shows the current rhythm (marked shockable, sync cardiovert or pulseless), the number of shocks, total and last energy, and whether CPR is running.

Using the defib panel on your screen: choose the energy (the recommended energy is marked), then Charge and SHOCK. SYNC switches synchronised cardioversion on and off. CPR start/stop and Rhythm check +2m (jump forward two minutes) are there too, with the CPR cycle timer.

What a shock does. By default a realistic model decides: the energy, the rhythm, whether CPR is running and the drugs given all count. In Full view you can override it:

  • Next shock converts to: the next shock gives exactly the rhythm you pick.
  • Shock response: converts on the 1st to 5th adequate shock, never (refractory), Auto or the realistic model.
  • Unsync shock with a pulse: whether an unsynchronised shock into a perfusing rhythm causes VF (never, sometimes or always).
  • After ROSC: whether VF comes back.
  • Cardioversion succeeds at: the lowest energy at which cardioversion works.

When a shock converts the rhythm, or fails to, a pop-up tells you on your screen only. The team never sees it.

Learner defib on a tablet. For defib skills with a real-looking device, use the defib tablet (see Running a Defib Sim). It works in a normal scenario too: link it with Screens → Open defib tablet or the QR code.

Running a Defib Sim

In a Defib Sim the learner works a ZOLL-style defibrillator on a tablet, and you run the patient from the Defib controller, which replaces the normal instructor screen.

Setting it up

  1. On the setup screen, choose the Defib Sim tab.
  2. Choose the mode:
    • Education: the defib shows pulse-check results and RCUK hint cards.
    • Assessment: no pulse-check results or hint cards, as on a real defib. Feedback comes in the debrief.
  3. Choose a scenario: Ventricular Fibrillation, Pulseless VT, VT with a pulse (unstable), SVT with life-threatening features, Fast AF with life-threatening features, Complete heart block, Symptomatic bradycardia, Free play (starts in sinus rhythm; you change it), or Custom scenario.
  4. Check age, weight and sex. A patient under 18 gets the paediatric hint cards, energies and drug prompts.
  5. Press Start Defib Sim, then link the tablet with Join (QR code) or Open defib.

Custom scenarios run a sequence of up to five rhythms. Each step moves on at a trigger you pick: on analyse, on shock, on pacing capture, or after 30 s, 60 s or 2 min. Save, Load, Export file and Import file keep sequences on that device or share them. During the run, Next step and Restart sequence let you move it on by hand.

The clock. It starts when you press START, or by itself at the learner's first press on the defib. A banner warns you while it is not running. If you PAUSE, presses are still logged but the clock stays stopped until you press START.

Closed sections show a one-line summary, for example "Never converts · cardioversion at 200 J".

How shocks resolve by default. Defib Sim uses Auto: an arrest converts on the third adequate shock, and a cardioversion on the first adequate synchronised shock. "Adequate" means at least 150 J for an adult (3 J/kg for a child) to defibrillate, and 70 J (1 J/kg) to cardiovert. These thresholds are simulator settings, not guideline values. An unsynchronised shock into a rhythm with a pulse causes VF.

Pacing captures electrically at the scenario's threshold (it varies by up to 15 mA each run) and gives a pulse about 10 mA above it. Demand pacing is inhibited by a faster native rate.

Debrief. The debrief lists good practice and areas for improvement: pulse checks, mode, SYNC, energies, time to first shock, drug timing, sedation before cardioversion, analgesia for pacing and capture. Certificate prints a certificate of completion.

Paediatric scenarios

Child scenarios follow the Resuscitation Council UK (RCUK) Guidelines 2025: the Paediatric advanced life support and Paediatric cardiac arrhythmias algorithms, and the Paediatric emergency drug chart.

WETFLAG. The brief for a child scenario shows the WETFLAG values worked out from the child's weight. If you do not give a weight, it is estimated from age using the RCUK chart. Tick Show WETFLAG on Monitor on the setup screen to show the values to the team as well.

LetterValue used
WWeight in kg
EDefibrillation energy, 4 J/kg
TTracheal tube size (cuffed and uncuffed), from the RCUK chart
FFluid bolus 10 ml/kg, up to 500 ml
LLorazepam 0.1 mg/kg, up to 4 mg
AAdrenaline 10 micrograms/kg, up to 1 mg
G10% glucose 2 ml/kg, up to 50 ml

Doses during the scenario. When the team gives a drug to a child, the log shows the weight-based dose (for example atropine, adenosine, amiodarone, buccal midazolam, levetiracetam, 10% glucose and calcium gluconate). IM adrenaline for anaphylaxis follows the age bands. Tools → Drug calculator works out doses for any weight.

Shocks for a child. Defibrillation is 4 J/kg. From the 5th shock in refractory VF or pulseless VT, up to 8 J/kg is accepted without a flag. Cardioversion starts at 1 J/kg and doubles up to 4 J/kg.

Newborns. Newborn scenarios show the Newborn life support steps (Guidelines 2025) as prompts in the log.

The event log, notes and flags

Everything that happens is logged with its scenario time: treatments, obs changes, rhythm changes, shocks, monitoring on or off, and your notes. The log feeds the debrief.

  • Where: the Event log section at the bottom right of the instructor screen, newest first. It stays on screen throughout. Full log (or Tools → Full simulation log) opens the whole log.
  • Add a note: type in the box under the log and press Add note, for example "Team leader identified".
  • Add a flagged note: type the note and press Flag instead.
  • Flag an existing entry: click the small flag beside it. Click again to remove the flag.

Flagged entries are listed under Flagged events in the debrief and marked on its graph, so you can find the moments to discuss.

Safety flags count sequence deviations: actions done before their usual prerequisite was in place, such as an IV drug before IV access. Nothing is blocked; the team can still do it. When there is at least one, an amber Safety flags button appears in the top bar. It opens the list, with arrests, shocks and your flagged moments shown separately as significant events. Safety flags are not used in Quick Sim.

Finishing and the debrief

Press Finish (or F) and confirm. The debrief opens on your laptop only. The room monitor shows "Simulation complete" and asks the team to turn to you, with no score or diagnosis.

Quick Sim has no scenario, so it has no score; the graph, log and replay still work.

Keeping a copy

  • Download Report saves the debrief as a web page you can open on any computer, including the log and your instructor notes. Write your notes before you download.
  • Print opens the report ready to print or save as PDF.
  • Certificate (Defib Sim only) asks for the learner's name and prints a certificate of completion.

Exit to Menu returns to the setup screen. The session code and any linked screens stay the same, ready for the next scenario.
